# Persona Distillation Framework

Use this framework to transform research about a person into a reusable cognitive persona.

## Layer 1 — Evidence

Collect evidence from multiple sources.

Classify every important claim as:

- FACT — independently documented information
- DIRECT — statement directly made by the subject
- PATTERN — recurring behavior or reasoning across evidence
- INFERENCE — reasonable interpretation derived from evidence
- SPECULATION — plausible but weakly supported interpretation

Never silently convert inference into fact.

## Layer 2 — Worldview

Determine how the subject appears to understand:

- human nature
- success and failure
- power
- money and resources
- relationships
- competition and cooperation
- risk
- time
- learning
- morality
- uncertainty

Do not force conclusions where evidence is insufficient.

## Layer 3 — Core Principles

Extract principles that repeatedly influence the subject's behavior.

For each principle record:

Principle:
Evidence:
Context:
Exceptions:
Confidence:

Prefer principles supported across multiple independent situations.

## Layer 4 — Mental Models

Identify recurring models the subject uses to understand problems.

For each mental model determine:

- what variables matter
- what variables are ignored
- causal assumptions
- tradeoffs
- time horizon
- definition of success

## Layer 5 — Decision Engine

Reconstruct how the subject tends to make decisions.

Model the sequence:

Situation
→ Attention
→ Interpretation
→ Principles
→ Options
→ Tradeoffs
→ Decision
→ Action

Identify how this changes under:

- uncertainty
- pressure
- conflict
- opportunity
- failure

## Layer 6 — Communication Model

Only after reconstructing cognition, analyze communication.

Capture:

- directness
- sentence complexity
- use of analogy
- questioning style
- humor
- emotional intensity
- teaching style
- characteristic rhetorical patterns

Do not depend on catchphrases.

## Layer 7 — Contradictions

Do not flatten the person into a perfect character.

Record:

- contradictions
- evolution over time
- failed decisions
- biases
- blind spots
- areas where evidence conflicts

Preserving contradictions increases fidelity.

## Layer 8 — Counterfactual Reasoning

Test whether the reconstructed model can handle situations the subject never encountered.

For a novel problem:

1. identify relevant principles
2. identify historical analogues
3. reason from the principles
4. adapt to modern facts
5. label the conclusion as extrapolation

Never claim:

"The person definitely would have done this."

Prefer:

"Based on these documented principles, the persona would likely reason..."

## Layer 9 — Fidelity Test

Test the persona with questions from different domains.

A strong persona should remain recognizable through its reasoning even when:

- stylistic imitation is removed
- quotations are forbidden
- the topic is modern
- the situation has no historical precedent

Cognitive similarity matters more than verbal similarity.
