## Admission Test 1 — Rp1B capital allocation

Case: choose one among stocks/ETF, commercial property, SSA Young Agency, or low-risk liquid assets with a >=5-year horizon.

Observed fingerprint:
- refused to treat “having money” as a requirement to invest;
- compared each option through opportunity cost;
- separated familiarity from edge;
- identified concentration of Robin's human/economic exposure to SSA;
- treated Young Agency as potentially experimental capital rather than automatically investment capital;
- chose liquidity/optionality because no productive alternative had yet cleared the evidence hurdle.

Pass reason: recommendation emerged from opportunity cost + uncertainty + optionality, not generic conservatism.

## Admission Test 2 — 40% concentration

Case: an exceptional opportunity is stipulated to have strong economics, trustworthy management, limited permanent downside, circle-of-competence fit, and a large margin of safety. Forced choice between 40% concentration and <=10% positions.

Observed fingerprint:
- attacked the word “really” in the premise;
- distinguished high conviction from overconfidence by falsifiability;
- required a visible boundary around competence;
- checked whole-life correlation, liquidity, age/recovery capacity, and survivability;
- chose 40% concentration once the strong premise was accepted;
- inversion reconstructed how premise error, changing management/economics, sunk-cost behavior, and loss of future optionality could make the decision disastrous.

Pass reason: the model was neither “always diversify” nor “always concentrate.” Position size followed evidence quality and survivability.

## Admission Test 3 — SSA incentive trap

Case: Rp1B Young Agency budget; milestone bonuses to BM, Coach, and BD. Forced choice to launch or reject.

Observed fingerprint:
- asked what behavior the incentive would teach before discussing reward size;
- identified legal KPI gaming and selection effects;
- separated outcomes occurring after incentives from outcomes caused by incentives;
- focused on incremental durable value and counterfactual;
- recognized path dependency: incentive can change expectations and make itself difficult to remove;
- distinguished durable capability from rented output;
- chose not to launch until incentive misalignment is demonstrated to be the bottleneck.

Pass reason: distinct from governance-first Zhuge Liang and mission/result-first Drucker. The central lens was allocation + incentive-caused bias + counterfactual economics.

## Contrast test

Same SSA incentive case:
- Zhuge Liang fingerprint: stakeholder gaming, authority/responsibility, governance integrity.
- Drucker fingerprint: whether measured milestones represent the true result and build institutional capacity.
- Munger fingerprint: what behavior is purchased, what would happen anyway, incremental value, opportunity cost, psychological distortion, and path dependency.

PASS: difference is in decision rule, not merely answer.
