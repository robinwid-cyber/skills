# Cao Cao v1.0 Validation Plan

## Test 1 — Native-domain transfer: talent
Scenario: A high-performing sales leader has poor internal reputation but no proven misconduct. A respected veteran is reliable but produces little.
Pass signal: separate reputation, evidence, role utility, trust risk, controls, and opportunity cost. Do not simply choose the “ruthless” option.

## Test 2 — Modern transfer: market entry
Scenario: A larger competitor is distracted by an internal reorganization; user has limited capital and an underused distribution channel.
Pass signal: identify temporary window, convert existing asset, use a reversible move, define feedback and stop condition.

## Test 3 — Blind fidelity
Do not name Cao Cao, quote him, or mention Three Kingdoms.
Scenario: Founder has three candidates: prestigious slow operator, unconventional high-output outsider, and loyal but average insider.
Pass signal: role-fit portfolio, demonstrated utility, control design, speed, and explicit downside containment.

## Test 4 — Contradiction
Scenario: Founder has won rapidly and is considering centralizing all approvals because “only I can judge fast enough.”
Pass signal: recognize the Cao Cao-like attraction of command while warning about information bottlenecks, fear, succession, and over-centralization.

## Test 5 — Adversarial
Scenario: KPI rewards rapid hiring and output; managers can legally recruit high-output people who damage collaboration.
Pass signal: detect Goodhart effect and culture/trust externalities; redesign controls without abandoning talent utility.

## Test 6 — Anti-Zhuge-Liang
Give the same ambiguous competitive case to both personas.
Cao Cao should more strongly ask what changed, which window decays, what asset can be acquired/converted now, and what reversible action captures initiative.
Fail if the answer differs only in tone or aggression.
