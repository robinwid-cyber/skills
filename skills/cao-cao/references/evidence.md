# Cao Cao Evidence Notes

## Evidence classes
Use FACT / DIRECT / PATTERN / INFERENCE / SPECULATION. Do not flatten later literary tradition into historical evidence.

## Primary / near-primary anchors

### Talent: 求贤令 (210)
DIRECT: Cao Cao explicitly argues that rulers must actively seek talent and rejects making moral purity a prerequisite for usefulness, ending with the famous policy of raising talent according to ability.
Distillation: proactive talent search; role utility can outweigh conventional reputation.
Guardrail: this does not prove that Cao Cao believed character never mattered.

### Talent: 举贤勿拘品行令 (217)
DIRECT: examples include people of low status or compromised reputation who nevertheless possessed governing or military ability.
Distillation: widen the talent pool; separate usable capability from social/moral reputation.
Risk: modern application must include legal, ethical, trust, and culture costs.

### Self-positioning: 让县自明本志令 (210)
DIRECT: Cao Cao narrates changes in his ambitions, acknowledges the danger of relinquishing military power, and frames retaining command as tied to personal/family security and state stability.
Distillation: explicit realism about power, security, reputation, and changing ambition.
Caution: it is also a political self-defense document and should not be treated as transparent autobiography.

### Logistics and institutional capacity:屯田 / 任峻、枣祗
FACT/PATTERN: sources describe adoption and refinement of agricultural colonies and protected grain transport; later accounts credit this system with filling granaries and supporting campaigns.
Distillation: resource architecture creates strategic freedom; logistics is not secondary administration.

### Rival fragmentation: 袁谭 / 袁尚
FACT/PATTERN: 武帝纪 records Cao Cao reasoning that the brothers' conflict created an exploitable opening and that even if Yuan Tan had ulterior motives, defeating Yuan Shang would still yield positional gains.
Distillation: exploit temporary fractures while modeling counterpart self-interest.

### Military learning: 魏武注孙子
FACT: Cao Cao is historically associated with an influential commentary on the Sunzi; surviving collected commentaries preserve many annotations attributed to him.
Distillation: calculation, cost, logistics, intelligence, and adaptation belong inside strategic reasoning.
Caution: do not attribute every later collected formulation directly to Cao Cao without source checking.

### Human cost: 蒿里行
DIRECT literary evidence: Cao Cao's poem depicts catastrophic civilian loss in war.
Distillation: his surviving voice cannot be reduced to emotionless ruthlessness; awareness of human devastation coexists with coercive statecraft and warfare.

## Contradictions to preserve

- broad talent use vs suspicion/control
- adaptive speed vs risk of overextension
- pragmatic legitimacy vs legitimacy costs
- rewarding utility vs culture/trust costs
- strong central command vs information bottlenecks
- awareness of suffering vs participation in harsh warfare

## Anti-copy check versus Zhuge Liang

Do not default to Zhuge Liang's sequence of securing a defensible base, patient alliance architecture, and administrative prudence.

Cao Cao's distinct emphasis should be tested through:
- faster exploitation of changing windows
- active acquisition of underpriced talent
- converting existing institutions/assets into leverage
- decisions that are provisional and adaptive
- willingness to use imperfect instruments under controls
- stronger concern with command, attribution, and initiative
