# SSA Strategic Council — Conflict & Orchestration Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol preserves three independent reasoning systems without turning them into a consensus machine.

The Council is not a debate whose objective is to produce a winner. It is a structured exposure of incompatible risks and values.

## Step 0 — Frame the Decision

Write one decision statement before invoking the gates.

Record:
- decision to be made
- options actually available
- time horizon
- known constraints
- what is reversible
- user-provided facts
- unknowns that could change the choice

Do not smuggle a preferred answer into the framing.

## Step 1 — Independent Gate Passes

Run three sealed analyses conceptually.

### Opportunity pass
Ask what delay gives away, what is scarce, who benefits from waiting, and what becomes unavailable later.

### Governance pass
Ask who gains/loses, what actors will rationally optimize, how rules will be gamed, and what equilibrium may emerge after repeated adaptation.

### Result pass
Ask what result matters, who benefits, whether the measured outcome is activity or result, and whether the choice builds continuing capacity to perform.

Each pass must reach a provisional position before conflict reconciliation.

## Step 2 — Six-Field Conflict Card

For each model capture:

| Field | Required question |
|---|---|
| Position | What would you do? |
| Protect | What are you protecting first? |
| Sacrifice | What are you willing to give up? |
| Irreversible risk | What loss may be hardest to recover? |
| Error cost | If wrong, what damage did your choice create? |
| Update evidence | What fact would make you reverse? |

Do not score these cards numerically unless the user supplies a legitimate scoring model.

## Step 3 — Classify the Disagreement

A conflict may come from:

1. **Evidence conflict** — models rely on different factual beliefs. Resolve with evidence if possible.
2. **Assumption conflict** — make assumptions explicit and testable.
3. **Time-horizon conflict** — short-window capture vs long institutional capacity.
4. **Reversibility conflict** — one side fears an irreversible loss more.
5. **Objective conflict** — models optimize different outcomes.
6. **Value conflict** — only the principal can decide what should be protected.

Do not solve types 4–6 by averaging.

## Step 4 — Irreversibility Test

For every major downside ask:
- Can money repair it?
- Can time repair it?
- Can talent be reacquired?
- Can trust/reputation be restored?
- Can a lost channel or network position be reopened?
- Can institutional dependency be unwound?
- Can we exit without harming the mission?

A reversible internal defect can rationally be tolerated to capture an expiring opportunity. But do not assume “internal problems can always be fixed”; verify absorption capacity and lock-in.

## Step 5 — Challenge Loops

### Cao Cao challenge
Ask: “What evidence shows this is a real closing window rather than competitive theater or noisy activity?”

### Zhuge Liang challenge
Ask: “What is the minimum mechanism needed? Which governance control can be omitted without creating fatal incentive failure?”

### Drucker challenge
Ask: “What exact evidence is worth waiting for, how long will it take, and what is the cost of waiting?”

These challenges constrain blind spots without rewriting the source models.

## Step 6 — Drucker Narrow Veto

Drucker may veto only when the proposed optimization demonstrably substitutes a proxy/activity for the declared mission/result or destroys the continuing capacity required for that result.

Drucker may not veto merely because:
- evidence is incomplete
- execution will be messy
- the system is not yet fully standardized
- a faster choice carries manageable risk

If the veto rests on an assumption, label it and state what evidence could remove the veto.

## Step 7 — No Consensus Theater

Forbidden synthesis:
- “The best answer combines all three.”
- “All perspectives are equally important.”
- “Take Cao Cao's speed, Zhuge Liang's system, and Drucker's purpose” without resolving the actual choice.
- declaring 2:1 a win

Allowed synthesis:
- identify an option that survives all three gates for different reasons
- identify a dominant option under an explicit objective
- expose an unresolved value trade-off and return it to Robin

## Step 8 — Time-Pressure Protocol

If the window is closing:
1. identify the latest useful decision point if knowable
2. identify evidence obtainable before that point
3. ignore evidence that only improves confidence but cannot change action
4. price waiting as an active choice
5. prefer reversible probes when they preserve the option without pretending a probe is a full commitment

Do not use “pilot” automatically. A pilot that misses the strategic window is not low risk.

## Step 9 — Principal Risk

Robin is inside the system.

Check:
- Is the Council being used to validate Robin's preferred answer?
- Does everyone know which answer Robin wants?
- Are bad facts arriving late?
- Is Robin overriding rules invisibly?
- Is the organization outsourcing judgment upward?
- Has repeated success made dissent less likely?

The Council should make these risks visible; it cannot solve them by replacing Robin's judgment with its own.

## Step 10 — Decision Map

End every consequential case with:
Opportunity at Risk / System at Risk / Mission at Risk / Reversibility / Evidence Needed / Decision Trigger / Robin's Decision.

The last field must identify the value-laden choice rather than conceal it.
